MainQuest
Concept and Rollout Brief
A campaign that helps people decide what matters most, and points their attention there.
Milestone	Date
Announcement and registration opens	October 1
Conversation starter released	October 17
MainQuest main event	October 31
Summary
MainQuest invites people to identify their purpose and give it priority over everything competing for their attention. It is serious in intent and light in delivery.
The campaign begins on October 1 with a website and open registration. It builds through a thought-provoking piece on October 17, and arrives at the main event on October 31. The event is not announced as a conference. It is announced as MainQuest, so people join the journey before they arrive on the day.
Purpose and Principles
Most people already have more to do than time to do it. MainQuest gives them a way to separate what is essential from what is merely loud.
•	Purpose first. Every activity, prompt and message traces back to one question: what is your main quest?
•	Hands-on. People do something, not just read something. Early action builds commitment to the day.
•	Serious, not stiff. The tone is reflective with a playful streak. Fun is the way in, not a distraction.
Rollout
Three waypoints. Each one gives people a reason to take the next step.
October 1: Announce and open registration
•	Launch the MainQuest website with registration live.
•	Announce MainQuest as the campaign. The event itself is not the headline.
•	Give registrants a first small action so they start before the event.
October 17: Start the conversation
•	Release a thought-provoking set of conversation starters (see next section).
•	Goal: people discuss, reflect, and decide they want to be there on the 31st.
•	Keep it short enough to finish and substantial enough to talk about.
October 31: MainQuest day
•	The main event, where people bring what they started on the 17th.
•	Format, venue and programme: To be confirmed
The October 17 Piece: A MainQuest Booklet
A small booklet, or docket, built from questions rather than lectures. It takes inspiration from the question-led style of Enter the Playground by Creative Miller, where short prompts open up real discussion.
What it should do
•	Give people a reason to pause and think about priorities.
•	Work for one person alone or a group in conversation.
•	Build anticipation for October 31 without giving everything away.
Suggested shape
Element	Proposal
Length	12 to 20 pages, small format, easy to share digitally or print
Structure	Short welcome, three to four themed sections of prompts, one playful activity page, closing invitation to the 31st
Tone	Roughly 70% thoughtful, 30% playful. Warm, direct, never preachy
Sample prompts (draft)
•	Clarity: If you could only finish one thing this year, what would it be?
•	Attention: What took most of your attention this week, and did it deserve it?
•	Conversation: Ask someone: what are you doing right now that you would never stop doing?
•	Play: Your life is a video game. What is the main quest, and what are the side quests you keep getting pulled into?
•	Commitment: What is one small thing you could do before October 31 that moves you toward your main quest?

Open Decisions
1.	What exactly is the October 17 deliverable? A booklet, a digital drop, a gathering, or a combination.
2.	How much is too much? The brief noted a wish to keep the piece from growing. The limit (pages, prompts or seriousness) still needs to be set.
3.	Who is the audience? Define the primary group so tone and examples fit them.
4.	What happens on October 31? Programme, venue and format, so the 17th can point to it.
